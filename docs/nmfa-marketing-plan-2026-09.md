## 1. The premise to challenge first

Eleven vacancies is not primarily a marketing spend problem, and buying more Zillow exposure before the pipe is fixed would pour leads into a bucket that is leaking at every joint. The evidence from our own systems over the last six weeks:

[Certain] Lead supply fell by half in late August: 14 leads in the seven days ending 08-25 against 28 the week before. The chatbot had written zero leads in 13 days with the widget confirmed live, Facebook was silent 21 days, Zillow silent 5 days. (Daily AI Audit, 2026-08-25.)

[Certain] A Zillow prospect replied to our auto-reply on July 28 asking "Do u have units at floor level for under 1000." That thread is still marked unread in the NMFA inbox on September 7. That is a hand-raiser who never got an answer for six weeks.

[Certain] Zapier flagged an error on the "Zillow Zap" on August 12 (Gmail returned a 400 while hydrating the email). Nobody has confirmed whether Zillow inquiries have flowed cleanly since.

[Certain] 286 leads sit in "Contacted" status on the dashboard as of today, with 2 to 7 new leads per day needing first contact. Contacted is where leads go to die unless someone owns the second and third touch.

[Certain] 70 percent of AI voice calls in the last 30 days captured no caller name, and 65 percent were filed as "No follow-up needed." A lead we cannot name cannot be called back.

[Certain] Our May competitive review scored NMFA at 0 of 4 on listing quality (no gym, no 3D tour, no listing boost, no special shown on Apartments.com), the smallest concession in the eight-property comp set, and a Google rating of 3.4 on 159 reviews, below five of eight comps. Both Apartments.com listings appeared dark on July 9 and their status has not been re-confirmed.

[Likely] Zillow's "3 leads year to date" figure that drove the July 13 decision to stay on Base is an undercount. 51 percent of year-to-date move-ins have no lead source in Rent Manager, Zillow was the single most under-credited source in the first-touch comparison (35 first-touch leads, 1 tagged), and the Zap error plus the unread reply show the Zillow email path itself drops leads. We declined the upgrade on numbers we now know we cannot trust.

So the order of operations is: stop the leaks in week one, fix the listing and the offer in weeks two through four, and only then scale paid exposure, Zillow included, against a scorecard that can actually tell us what worked.

## 2. Market context

[Certain, per Kidder Mathews and Northmarq Q2 2026 reports] Phoenix metro vacancy was 11.3 percent in Q2 2026, down 40 basis points year over year. Average asking rent was $1,536, down 2.2 percent year over year, with two-bedroom averaging $1,632. Absorption outpaced deliveries in the first half, and deliveries in the second half of 2026 are expected to be roughly half of last year's second half. Rent growth is slightly negative, concessions are common (one source puts it above half of communities), but the supply tailwind is turning in our favor for the first time since 2022.

[Certain] NMFA at 11 of 194 is 5.7 percent vacant, half the metro rate. We are not underperforming the market. The gap is against Michel's own trajectory (below 10 after June), and every vacant unit costs about $1,295 a month in all-in rent, so 11 vacancies is roughly $14,000 a month of lost revenue.

[Certain] NMFA's live price is $1,295 all-in on a six-month term for a two-bedroom, 880 square feet, with electric, water, sewer and trash included, and $1,750 for a three-bedroom. The 12-month term is $1,500 all-in with $750 off the deposit. Solaris ($1,299), Rise Desert Cove ($1,295), Element ($1,279) and Granite Bay ($1,135) all list their two-bedrooms without utilities, which the May deck valued at $120 to $180 a month. On the six-month term, NMFA is now the cheapest true monthly cost on the street. Nobody outside this office knows that, because "all utilities included" is not in our headline price anywhere.

[Likely] Timing matters. September and October are the fall moving window in Phoenix; demand thins from mid-November through the holidays. Whatever we launch needs to be live by mid-September to catch it.

## 3. The math the plan has to hit

Target: 11 vacant to 6 or fewer by November 1, and under 5 by December 15, net of move-outs. That means about 5 net new leases in the next 55 days, plus enough leases to cover whatever expires in the same window.

[Certain, industry benchmarks] Lead-to-lease conversion averages 8.7 percent nationally, with 10 to 15 percent considered good and top performers above 16.5 percent. At 10 percent, 5 leases need 50 qualified NMFA leads, about one per day, roughly double the current NMFA pace. At 15 percent, 34 leads. So the plan needs both more volume and better conversion; either alone will fall short.

[Certain] Speed to lead is the cheapest conversion lever we have. Contact within five minutes makes a lead roughly 21 times more likely to qualify than contact at 30 minutes, and 78 percent of buyers go with the first responder. The AI lines already answer instantly. The gap is the human second touch on chat, Zillow and Facebook leads, which today can take days.

## 4. Zillow: upgrade or not

What Zillow's Signature package includes, per Zillow's own product pages and case study [Certain that Zillow claims this, Likely that it holds for a 194-unit Class C property]:

Everything in Premium (prioritized placement across Zillow, Trulia and HotPads), plus extended syndication to Realtor.com, Redfin, Rent. and ApartmentGuide, standout map icons, professional media credits for Zillow 3D Home tours, interactive floor plans and high-resolution photography, retargeting through email, push notifications, and TikTok, Facebook and Instagram ads, and placement inside Zillow's AI mode and the Google Gemini connected app that launched in July 2026. Zillow's ZRS Management case study reports about 50 percent more impressions, triple the total prospects and a 71 percent lift in direct leads versus Base, across a pilot that grew from 12 to 67 communities. That is Zillow marketing copy about a large institutional operator, so treat it as the ceiling, not the expectation.

[Certain] Multifamily package pricing is not published. It is quoted by a Zillow rep per property and negotiable. The $29.99 per listing and $39.99 premium figures online are the small-landlord Rental Manager product, not our tier.

[Guessing] One aggregator claims Zillow's multifamily model bills roughly $900 per signed lease rather than per lead. Confirm with the rep before believing it; if true, a pay-per-lease structure is low risk for us because we only pay when a unit fills.

Two Zillow features matter more than the tier and cost nothing:

Total Price display. Zillow now shows an all-in monthly figure with a "Total Price" badge when the provider supplies fee data, so comps that list $1,299 base plus utilities and fees get exposed while NMFA's $1,295 already includes everything. [Likely] The feature is rolling out beyond the states that mandate it; ask the rep to enable it on NMFA now. If it is available in Arizona, it is the single best way to make our utilities-included pricing visible in a search result.

AI Assist. Zillow's own AI (built on EliseAI) will answer inquiries and book tours 24/7, and Zillow reports 25 percent higher lead-to-tour on leads it engages. That overlaps with our own Zillow auto-reply. Pick one: either Zillow AI Assist owns the first reply and hands booked tours to us, or our auto-reply does and we shut AI Assist off. Running both creates two voices answering one prospect.

Recommendation: request a Signature quote for NMFA only and run a 90-day pilot starting October 1, after the week-one fixes below, with these conditions written down before signing. Zillow must report leads, tours and leases from its own dashboard, not our inbox. Zillow gets a dedicated tracking phone number that forwards to the NMFA AI line (520-600-6936) so calls are attributed. Every Zillow lead gets a source tag in wp_paa_leads and Rent Manager. The kill criterion is a cost per signed lease above $1,000 at day 90, which is the upper end of the published ILS benchmark range ($607 to $1,005). If Zillow will not report leads at the property level, do not sign; that was the real problem in July and it has not changed.

Do this in parallel: confirm whether the Apartments.com listing is actually dark, and if so restore it before spending a dollar on Zillow. The May comp set was built from Apartments.com and Zillow audience overlap, which means our prospects are on both. Third-party price guides put Apartments.com Silver near $199 and Gold near $399 per property per month [Guessing, not confirmed with CoStar].

Confirm the Apartments.com listing status by phone with CoStar. Owner: Jenea.

Work the "Contacted" backlog. Pull every NMFA lead from the last 90 days with a phone number or email, and send one re-engagement message in English and Spanish with the current price, the utilities-included line, and the tour link. Email through Mailchimp is fine today. Texting requires prior consent, so only text leads whose consent is recorded. Owner: Felipa and Stephany, list from Jenea.

### Weeks 2 to 4 (September 15 to October 5): fix the listing and the offer

Rewrite the listing headline on every channel to lead with the number a renter compares: "$1,295 a month, all utilities included, 2 bed, 880 sq ft." Put the same line in Spanish. Post the special on every channel at once so a prospect who sees Zillow and Apartments.com sees the same offer.

Shoot a 3D tour of the model two-bedroom and one three-bedroom. Zillow's 3D Home app is free on a phone and syndicates to Realtor.com; Matterport is the alternative if Apartments.com is restored. The May deck called this the easiest quick win and it is still not done. Add a floor plan to every listing.

Decide the concession. This is Michel's call, and the current offer ($750 off the deposit on a 12-month lease, nothing on six months) was the weakest in the comp set in May and still is. Frame it as vacancy cost: one month free on $1,295 spread across a 12-month lease is $108 a month, and a unit that leases 30 days sooner has already paid for it. Options in rising cost order: waive application and admin fees (Rise Desert Cove's play, about $200 to $300 of value); half off the first month (about $650); one month free on 12 months (about $1,295, matches Granite Bay and Element). Whatever is chosen, give it a deadline on the listing, because Rise Desert Cove showed a hard date creates urgency a standing offer does not.

Google Business Profile. Respond to every review from the last 12 months, positive and negative, this month. Communities that answer more than 90 percent of reviews see measurably more profile views and direction requests. Post the special as a Google update weekly, confirm the phone number and hours, and add the 3D tour and new photos. Launch the move-in and renewal review asks that are already planned, sent to every resident the same way with no filtering, since review gating is prohibited by Google policy and by an FTC rule with financial penalties.

Resident referral. [Certain] Arizona Revised Statutes 32-2176 permits an apartment tenant to receive a finder fee as a credit toward rent, and the version currently posted no longer carries the $200 cap that the 2011 text had. [Likely] A $300 to $500 rent credit paid after the referred resident completes 60 days is inside the norm and inside the statute. Have counsel confirm the current cap before announcing it. Announce it in English and Spanish by door flyer and email; a full building of 183 households is the cheapest lead source we have.

Facebook Marketplace. Post each vacant unit as its own listing with the AI text line CTA already used in the descriptions, in English and Spanish, and refresh weekly. Marketplace is free and its inbox has no API, so a human checks it daily until the Meta webhook is confirmed live on real prospects.

Zillow. Request the Signature quote, ask for Total Price and the AI Assist decision in the same call, and set the pilot start for October 1 with the tracking conditions in Section 4.

Google Ads. The July 23 consultant review was to confirm which conversion action the Performance Max campaign optimizes toward and whether GA4 conversions land in wp_paa_leads with attribution. If that is still unconfirmed, confirm it before adding budget. [Certain, RentVision and WordStream 2026 benchmarks] Multifamily search CPC averages about $2.23 and real estate cost per lead about $102, so $1,000 a month of well-targeted NMFA-only spend should produce roughly 10 leads, or about one lease. Worth it only once tracking is trustworthy.

Prospect drip. The Twilio drip (Day 0, 2, 5, 9) is built and still in DRY_RUN. Turn it on for new leads with recorded consent only, after wiring sms-webhook.php to mark a lead "replied" so a prospect who answers stops receiving nudges. Without that hook, a live drip will text people who have already replied.

### Weeks 5 to 12 (October 6 to November 30): scale what works

Run one weekly scorecard by source: leads, tours, applications, leases, and cost per lease. The dashboard already has most of the counts; the missing piece is source tagging discipline in Rent Manager, which is also what makes the Zillow decision at day 90 honest.

Shift budget every two weeks toward the two sources with the lowest cost per lease and starve the rest.

On December 29, decide Zillow Signature on its own numbers: continue, renegotiate, or drop back to Base.
